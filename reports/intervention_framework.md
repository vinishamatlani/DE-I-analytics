# Inclusion Intervention Framework

## Purpose

The following interventions are proposed as portfolio-level
examples of how an organisation could respond to inclusion
analytics.

They are not presented as proven solutions from the synthetic
dataset.

---

## Intervention 1 — Strengthen Psychological Safety

### Objective

Create stronger conditions for employees to raise concerns,
challenge existing practices, and ask for help without fear of
unfair consequences.

### Potential actions

- Manager training on inclusive team behaviours
- Structured team check-ins
- Anonymous listening channels
- Team retrospectives focused on speaking up
- Manager follow-up expectations after concerns are raised

### Measures

**Primary**

- Psychological Safety score
- Psychological Safety favourable %

**Secondary**

- Employee Voice score
- Concern-raising comfort
- Segment-level psychological safety gaps

### Review cadence

Quarterly

---

## Intervention 2 — Strengthen Employee Voice

### Objective

Increase the extent to which employee perspectives are heard,
considered, and followed through.

### Potential actions

- Employee listening sessions
- Structured feedback channels
- Quarterly pulse surveys
- "You said / We did" communication
- Cross-functional employee forums

### Measures

**Primary**

- Employee Voice score
- Employee Voice favourable %

**Secondary**

- Survey participation
- Number of feedback themes receiving follow-up
- Perceived responsiveness of leadership

### Review cadence

Quarterly

---

## Intervention 3 — Improve Fairness & Opportunity

### Objective

Improve perceptions of equitable access to career development
and fair treatment across employee groups.

### Potential actions

- Transparent career frameworks
- Structured promotion criteria
- Career-development access reviews
- Mentorship and sponsorship programmes
- Regular review of segment-level opportunity gaps

### Measures

**Primary**

- Fairness & Opportunity score
- Fairness & Opportunity favourable %

**Secondary**

- Career-development participation
- Promotion fairness perception
- Segment-level inclusion gaps
- Participation in mentorship programmes

### Review cadence

Quarterly or semi-annually

---

# Measurement Principle

Interventions should be evaluated using repeated measurement.

A simple evaluation framework is:

Baseline
→ intervention
→ follow-up measurement
→ compare change
→ investigate segment differences
→ adjust intervention

Changes in survey scores should not automatically be interpreted
as causal evidence unless the evaluation design supports that claim.
